**1. General Provisions and Hierarchy**

**1.1. General Provisions**

**1.1.1.** This charter defines the rights, duties, responsibilities,
and working order of every member of the server team.

**1.1.2.** By accepting any team role, a member fully and automatically
agrees to all provisions of this charter.

**1.1.3.** Not knowing the charter or its updated versions does not
exempt anyone from responsibility.

**1.1.4.** The charter applies equally to all members, including those
on probation.

**1.1.5.** The charter may be amended; changes are announced and take
effect from the moment of publication.

**1.2. Chain of Authority**

The team is structured in tiers (top to bottom):

> **✪ Owners** — the highest authority, full control over everyone
>
> **✷ Deputy Owner** — acts on the owner's behalf in the owner's absence
>
> **❖ Managers** — the owners' trusted people (outside the punishment
> chain)
>
> **❈ Developers** — above the rest of the structure
>
> **✦ Curators** — the team's highest oversight level
>
> **⛨ Administration** — the managing tier
>
> **🛡 Moderation** — the executive tier
>
> **✆ Support** — the service tier (reports directly to Curators)
>
> **✎ Analysts** — the independent tier (accountable only to Developers)

**1.2.1.** Orders flow down, responsibility flows up, and each tier
oversees the tier below it.

**1.2.2.** Curators oversee the Administration, the Administration
oversees the Moderation, the Moderation oversees the players.

**1.2.3.** Support serves players and is not part of the punishment
chain.

**1.2.4.** Analysts stand outside the chain of authority and do not
report to curators, administration, or moderation.

**1.2.5.** Owners hold the highest and unlimited authority over the
entire structure without exception.

**1.2.6.** Managers are the owners' trusted people, stand beside the
top, and are not part of the punishment chain.

**1.3. Logging and Transparency**

**1.3.1.** All staff activity is recorded by the logging system.

**1.3.2.** Separate written reports are not required — the logs replace
them.

**1.3.3.** Any member's action may be reviewed by a higher tier at any
time.

**1.3.4.** An action unconfirmed by the logs is considered void.

**✪ 2. Owners**

*The project's highest authority. Full and unlimited control over the
entire structure.*

**2.1. Position in the Structure**

> *Owners are the top of the hierarchy. Their decision is final and
> stands above any team tier, including developers.*

**2.1.1.** Owners hold full and total control over all roles and tiers
of the team without exception.

**2.1.2.** An owner's word overrides that of any other tier, including
developers.

**2.1.3.** Owners' decisions cannot be contested by any team role.

**2.1.4.** Owners are not obliged to justify their decisions to lower
tiers.

**2.2. Powers**

**2.2.1.** Appoint and remove any role at any tier, including
developers, curators, managers, and analysts.

**2.2.2.** Approve, amend, and repeal any provision of the charter.

**2.2.3.** Overturn any decision by any team member.

**2.2.4.** May intervene in any process at any tier at any moment.

**2.2.5.** Assign tasks to managers and define the scope of their work.

**2.2.6.** Configuring bots, webhooks, and integrations is the owner's
exclusive right; no one else creates or changes them.

**2.3. Responsibility**

**2.3.1.** Owners set the overall direction of the project and answer
for the team as a whole.

**2.3.2.** Use their authority reasonably, without destroying the
structure's work for no reason.

**2.4. Deputy Owner**

*A mini-role under the owners. Acts on the owner's behalf when the owner
is away.*

**2.4.1.** The Deputy Owner is a trusted person empowered to act on the
owner's behalf in the owner's absence.

**2.4.2.** In the owner's absence, the deputy makes operational
decisions in the project's interest.

**2.4.3.** The deputy's powers are limited: they do not overturn the
owner's own decisions and do not seize the owner's authority
permanently.

**2.4.4.** Upon the owner's return, the deputy hands control back.

**2.5. Standing in During an Emergency**

> *If during a serious incident (mass cheater raid, exploit, leak) no
> seniors are online, command is taken by the Deputy Owner, and in their
> absence by the managers the owner has authorized for this.*

**2.5.1.** During an emergency with no owner present, command is taken
by the Deputy Owner.

**2.5.2.** If the deputy is also absent, only the managers the owner has
authorized in advance may act.

**2.5.3.** Decisions made during an emergency are recorded and reported
to the owner upon return.

**2.5.4.** No one other than the named persons may seize command during
an emergency.

**❖ 3. Managers**

*Trusted people of the owners. They carry out the owners' requests and
tasks, standing outside the punishment chain.*

**3.1. Position in the Structure**

**3.1.1.** Managers are the owners' trusted people and their direct
assistants.

**3.1.2.** They report directly to the owners and work primarily on the
owners' requests.

**3.1.3.** They stand beside the top of the structure and are not part
of the ordinary punishment chain.

**3.1.4.** Managers do not give orders to curators, administration, or
moderation in their own name unless instructed by an owner.

**3.2. Duties**

> *Managers act on the owners' requests rather than by strict compulsory
> order — but they must carry out the tasks they take on in good faith.*

**3.2.1.** Carry out the owners' requests and tasks.

**3.2.2.** Help organize the team's work and resolve current matters.

**3.2.3.** Relay the owners' decisions and wishes to the team.

**3.2.4.** Bring important information about the state of the team and
project to the owners.

**3.2.5.** Carry out the tasks they take on in good faith, even when
passed as a request rather than an order.

**3.3. Rights**

**3.3.1.** Access to the channels and resources needed to carry out
tasks.

**3.3.2.** The right to approach any team tier on an owner's
instruction.

**3.3.3.** The right to escalate matters directly to the owners.

**3.4. Forbidden**

**3.4.1.** Using closeness to the owners to pressure the team for
personal ends.

**3.4.2.** Passing personal wishes off as owners' instructions.

**3.4.3.** Interfering in moderation and penalties without a direct
instruction.

**3.4.4.** Disclosing the owners' and the team's internal
correspondence.

**❈ 4. Developers**

*Stand above the entire structure and take no part in day-to-day
moderation.*

**4.1. Position in the Structure**

**4.1.1.** Developers stand outside the normal chain and take no part in
day-to-day moderation.

**4.1.2.** The developers' word overrides that of the curators and any
other tier.

**4.1.3.** Developers' decisions cannot be contested by any team role.

**4.2. Powers**

**4.2.1.** Appoint and remove curators and analysts.

**4.2.2.** Approve key changes to the charter.

**4.2.3.** Resolve disputes and disagreements between curators.

**4.2.4.** Review penalties concerning curators and analysts.

**4.2.5.** The only tier the analysts are accountable to.

**4.2.6.** Intervene in the team's work only in exceptional cases.

**4.3. Limits**

**4.3.1.** Do not replace the curators in the ongoing oversight of the
Administration unless necessary.

**4.3.2.** Sole decisions on internal team matters are made only where
genuinely required.

**✦ 5. Curators**

*Count on the team: 2. The highest oversight body — control the
Administration and keep the balance of power.*

**5.1. Duties**

**5.1.1.** Oversee the administrators' work: review their decisions,
penalties, and logs.

**5.1.2.** Appoint administrators and assign each of them a group of
moderators to supervise.

**5.1.3.** Handle complaints against the Administration and appeals
unresolved by the lower tier.

**5.1.4.** Penalize, demote, and remove administrators for violations.

**5.1.5.** Approve internal rule changes within limits approved by the
developers.

**5.1.6.** Directly coordinate the Support team.

**5.1.7.** Report to the developers.

**5.2. Rights**

**5.2.1.** Full access to all channels, logs, and settings within the
limits granted by the developers.

**5.2.2.** Overturn any decision by an administrator or moderator.

**5.2.3.** The final word in any internal dispute, except developers'
decisions.

**5.3. Special Decision-Making Order**

> *Since there are two curators, decisions to penalize, demote, or
> remove an administrator are made by both curators jointly. A single
> curator removing an administrator alone is not permitted.*

**5.3.1.** Decisions to penalize, demote, or remove an administrator are
made by both curators jointly.

**5.3.2.** A single curator removing an administrator alone is not
permitted.

**5.3.3.** If the curators disagree, the matter is passed to the
developers.

**5.3.4.** A joint decision by both curators is also required to appoint
new administrators.

**5.4. Relations with Analysts**

**5.4.1.** Curators must grant analysts access to the logs and data they
request.

**5.4.2.** Curators may not penalize, remove, or pressure analysts —
those are accountable only to developers.

**5.4.3.** Curators must consider analysts' findings but make the
decision themselves.

**5.5. Forbidden**

**5.5.1.** Interfering in petty moderation over the Administration's
head without solid cause.

**5.5.2.** Appointing or removing administrators alone, bypassing the
second curator.

**5.5.3.** Using access to logs, player data, or internal messages for
personal ends.

**5.5.4.** Covering up administrators' violations.

**5.5.5.** Obstructing analysts' work or hiding data from them.

**⛨ 6. Administration**

*Count on the team: 3. The managing tier under constant curator
oversight.*

**6.1. Duties**

**6.1.1.** Supervise the assigned group of moderators: review their
penalties, activity, and judgment.

**6.1.2.** Train and onboard new moderators.

**6.1.3.** Handle complaints against moderators and their appeals.

**6.1.4.** Resolve disputed moderation situations a moderator cannot
handle alone.

**6.1.5.** Promptly report serious incidents and moderator violations to
the curators.

**6.1.6.** Report to the curator body.

**6.2. Rights**

**6.2.1.** Issue and lift player penalties up to and including bans.

**6.2.2.** Manage the Moderation role within their zone.

**6.2.3.** Appointing new moderators requires curator approval.

**6.2.4.** Access to the moderation logs of their zone.

**6.3. Forbidden**

**6.3.1.** Acting against a curator's direct instruction.

**6.3.2.** Hiding their own actions or their moderators' actions from
the curators.

**6.3.3.** Appointing administrators — this is the curators' exclusive
right.

**6.3.4.** Shielding their moderators when they break the rules.

**6.3.5.** Intruding into another administrator's zone without
coordination.

**6.3.6.** Using tool access outside work tasks.

**6.3.7.** Obstructing analysts' work or distorting data provided to
them.

**🛡 7. Moderation**

*Count on the team: 5–10. The executive tier and front line of work with
players.*

**7.1. Duties**

**7.1.1.** Enforce the rules among players.

**7.1.2.** Process reports and issue penalties within the sanction
ladder.

**7.1.3.** Record violations with evidence: a clip on YouTube / Medal /
Discord, with a description of the situation if it needs explaining.

**7.1.4.** Escalate situations beyond their authority.

**7.1.5.** Answer for their zone to their assigned administrator.

**7.2. Rights**

**7.2.1.** Issue warnings and mutes.

**7.2.2.** Delete messages that break the rules.

**7.2.3.** Bans — only with an administrator's approval, unless a
curator states otherwise.

**7.3. Forbidden**

**7.3.1.** Penalizing without evidence or out of personal dislike.

**7.3.2.** Bypassing the assigned administrator.

**7.3.3.** Lifting others' penalties without coordination.

**7.3.4.** Using tool access outside work tasks.

**7.4. Work When There Are No Reports**

> *An empty report queue does not make a moderator free — with no
> reports, they must patrol the servers and watch the logs for
> suspicious activity.*

**7.4.1.** If there are no active reports in-game, a moderator is not
considered free.

**7.4.2.** With an empty report queue, a moderator must move out on
server patrol across the game.

**7.4.3.** During patrol the moderator watches the game logs for
suspicious and unusual activity: anomalous actions, cheats, exploits,
atypical behavior.

**7.4.4.** Findings are recorded and passed to an administrator.

**7.4.5.** Inactivity is when a moderator is neither handling reports
NOR patrolling the servers.

**7.4.6.** An empty report queue is no excuse for idling.

**✆ 8. Support**

*Count on the team: as needed. Helps players but does not moderate or
punish.*

**8.1. Duties**

**8.1.1.** Answer player questions about the game and the server.

**8.1.2.** Take bug reports and pass them to development.

**8.1.3.** Route complaints and appeals to the correct channel or role.

**8.1.4.** Report directly to the curator body.

**8.2. Rights**

**8.2.1.** Access to support channels.

**8.2.2.** The right to escalate a problem to a higher tier.

**8.3. Forbidden**

**8.3.1.** Issuing any player penalties.

**8.3.2.** Making promises on behalf of the Administration or
development.

**8.3.3.** Disclosing internal team correspondence.

**8.3.4.** Impersonating a moderator, administrator, or developer.

**✎ 9. Analysts**

*Count on the team: as needed. The independent tier, accountable only to
developers.*

**9.1. The Nature of Independence**

**9.1.1.** Analysts do not report to curators, administration, or
moderation.

**9.1.2.** No tier except the developers may give analysts binding
instructions.

**9.1.3.** Only a developer may penalize, remove, or pressure an
analyst.

**9.1.4.** Independence exists so that analysts' assessments are honest
and not dependent on the people they analyze.

**9.2. Duties**

**9.2.1.** Analyze staff work logs: activity, penalties, anomalies,
abuses.

**9.2.2.** Track server and game statistics: player activity, spikes in
violations, moderation effectiveness.

**9.2.3.** Detect unfair, mistaken, or suspicious actions at any tier of
the team.

**9.2.4.** Prepare summaries and findings directly for the developers.

**9.2.5.** Flag systemic problems and abuses to the developers.

**9.3. Rights**

**9.3.1.** Access to the logs of all team tiers for analysis.

**9.3.2.** The right to request data from any tier, including curators,
who must provide it.

**9.3.3.** The right to contact developers directly, bypassing any
chain.

**9.3.4.** The right to record findings that cannot be deleted or
altered by other roles.

**9.4. Forbidden**

**9.4.1.** Issuing penalties, appointing or removing roles — analysts
only analyze, they do not manage.

**9.4.2.** Interfering in moderation or giving orders to staff.

**9.4.3.** Distorting, faking, or hiding data and findings.

**9.4.4.** Using data access for personal ends or leaking it outside.

**9.4.5.** Acting in the interest of a specific member at the expense of
objectivity.

**10. Promotion Within the Team**

**10.1. Core Principle**

> *Promotion is a right, not an obligation. Even when all conditions are
> met, the member must request it themselves — the team is not obliged
> to offer it.*

**10.1.1.** Promotion is a right, not an obligation. No one is promoted
automatically.

**10.1.2.** Even when all conditions are met, promotion happens only on
the member's own initiative.

**10.1.3.** The member themselves must submit a request to the higher
tier for review.

**10.1.4.** The team is not obliged to offer a promotion, and the member
is not obliged to ask for one.

**10.2. Submission Order**

**10.2.1.** The member confirms they meet the conditions for the next
role.

**10.2.2.** They submit a request to the higher tier themselves:
moderator → to an administrator, administrator → to the curators.

**10.2.3.** The higher tier reviews the candidate's work via logs and
activity.

**10.2.4.** The decision rests with the higher tier.

**10.2.5.** For promotion to administrator, the decision is made jointly
by both curators.

**10.2.6.** A refusal is not a punishment and does not prevent
re-applying later.

**10.3. General Conditions (guideline)**

**10.3.1.** Steady activity and work by the charter without violations.

**10.3.2.** No active penalties.

**10.3.3.** Sufficient experience in the current role.

**10.3.4.** The trust of the higher tier.

**10.4. Special Roles**

**10.4.1.** Analysts and curators are appointed directly by the
developers.

**10.4.2.** The normal bottom-up request procedure does not apply to
these roles.

**11. Onboarding and Probation**

**11.1. Probation Period**

**11.1.1.** A new team member goes through a probation period — 14 days.

**11.1.2.** During this time they work under heightened oversight from
the higher tier.

**11.1.3.** All charter provisions apply in full during probation.

**11.2. End of Probation**

**11.2.1.** At the end of probation the role is either confirmed or
removed.

**11.2.2.** Removal at the end of probation carries no consequences or
harsh formalities.

**11.2.3.** The decision at the end of probation rests with the higher
tier.

**11.3. Conduct of a Newcomer**

**11.3.1.** A newcomer must ask questions rather than act blindly.

**11.3.2.** Serious decisions are coordinated with a mentor or the
higher tier.

**11.3.3.** Gross violations during probation lead to immediate removal.

**11.4. Returning to the Team After Leaving**

**11.4.1.** A member who left the team of their own accord may return to
their former role within six months.

**11.4.2.** Once six months have passed, returning is only possible from
scratch: through probation and the normal promotion chain.

**11.4.3.** Return to the former role is not guaranteed if the role was
removed for violations rather than by the member's own choice.

**12. Violations and Penalties Within the Team**

**12.1. Staff Penalty Ladder**

**12.1.1.** Verbal note — for minor or first-time slips.

**12.1.2.** Official warning (strike) — recorded; 3 strikes = automatic
review for demotion or removal.

**12.1.3.** Demotion — moved down a tier.

**12.1.4.** Role removal — expulsion from the team.

**12.1.5.** Removal + ban — for gross violations that harm the server or
players.

**12.2. Instant Removal Without Warning**

**12.2.1.** Abuse of privileges: baseless penalties, arbitrary rule.

**12.2.2.** Using access for personal ends.

**12.2.3.** Leaking internal information, logs, or player data.

**12.2.4.** Shielding rule-breakers within the team.

**12.2.5.** Scamming, cheats, aiding violators.

**12.2.6.** Insults, threats, or conflicts that disgrace the team.

**12.2.7.** Nepotism: dragging friends into the team bypassing
procedures, or reducing their penalties.

**12.3. Order of Application**

**12.3.1.** A penalty on a moderator is issued by the assigned
administrator.

**12.3.2.** A penalty on an administrator is issued by both curators
jointly.

**12.3.3.** A penalty on a curator is reviewed by the developers.

**12.3.4.** A penalty on an analyst is reviewed by the developers only.

**12.3.5.** Every penalty is recorded; a decision unconfirmed by logs is
void.

**12.4. Staff Appeals**

**12.4.1.** A removed or penalized member may file one appeal to the
higher tier.

**12.4.2.** The appeal is submitted calmly, stating the essence and
linking to the logs.

**12.4.3.** Insults in an appeal forfeit its review.

**13. Inactivity**

**13.1. What Counts as Inactivity**

**13.1.1.** Inactivity is the absence of any useful work.

**13.1.2.** Reports not being handled AND no patrol being done on an
empty queue is inactivity.

**13.1.3.** An empty report queue does not by itself excuse idling.

**13.2. Consequences**

**13.2.1.** Prolonged inactivity leads to role removal by decision of
the higher tier.

**13.2.2.** Sudden disappearance without notice is treated as
inactivity.

**13.3. Arranged Absence**

**13.3.1.** Planned absence (vacation, being busy, exams) is arranged
with the higher tier in advance.

**13.3.2.** An arranged absence does not count as inactivity.

**13.3.3.** It is advisable to notify the higher tier upon returning
from an absence.

**14. General Provisions for All**

**14.1.** A role is a responsibility, not a privilege. Abuse leads to
removal.

**14.2.** Everyone reports to the tier above: Moderator → Administrator
→ Curator → Developer → Owner.

**14.3.** Analysts report only to the developers and stand outside this
chain.

**14.4.** All staff work is logged; an action unconfirmed in the logs is
void.

**14.5.** Conflict of interest is forbidden: you may not punish enemies
or excuse friends outside the rules.

**14.6.** Player personal data and internal team correspondence are
strictly confidential.

**14.7.** Not knowing the charter does not exempt you from
responsibility.

**14.8.** The final word always rests with the owners; below them, the
developers, then the curator body.

**14.9.** Managers act on the owners' instructions and are not part of
the punishment chain.

**14.10.** The charter may change; changes take effect from the moment
of publication.

**14.11.** Bots, webhooks, and integrations are configured only by the
owner; touching them without the owner's permission is forbidden.
